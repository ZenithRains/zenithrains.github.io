I began climbing Mount Fuji before dawn on September 6, 2026.

First, a few practical notes about renting and returning equipment, in case they are useful to someone making the trip.

I rented my gear from Yamarent. Its Kawaguchiko shop is about twenty minutes on foot from Kawaguchiko Station, or four to five minutes by bicycle. There is a bus stop beside the shop where the M-line service to Mount Fuji's Fifth Station stops. To be on the safe side, though, I would suggest returning to Kawaguchiko Station to catch the bus.

Returning the equipment brought an unexpected complication. The weather was severe on September 7, and Yamarent's main Shinjuku shop was closed. Fortunately, the equipment bag came with a red Yamato Transport waybill for shipping with the charges paid by the recipient. I could take the bag to a nearby Yamato service counter and send it back. I paid no additional shipping fee for this return.

My main lesson about equipment is simple: bring the full kit. Things that seem unlikely to be useful before departure often prove their worth in bad weather. On this trip, nothing felt superfluous.

After resting at Kusushi Shrine on the crater rim, I still wanted to continue to Kengamine. I met another climber and asked him which way to go. He said:

> “It's nothing to see, no go.”

There was nothing to see. Better not to go.

I still wanted to look a little farther, so I walked a few dozen metres towards Kengamine. The crater was a blank expanse of mist. I continued uphill for a short distance, into wind and driving rain. That was enough to make the decision clear. I turned back and began descending, without the slightest regret.

On this trip, the descent on the Yoshida Trail felt harder to endure than the climb. Switchback followed switchback, as though the same stretch of path had been repeated countless times. In clear weather, with distant views opening up ahead, I imagine the descent would be enjoyable. In wind and rain, with little to see, every turn looked like the one I had just passed.

I would walk for what felt like a long time, take out my phone to check my progress, and discover that there was still a very, very long way to go. Put the phone away. Keep walking. A little later, take it out again.

If I were to leave a few words for this climb, I would borrow the closing lines of the *Iroha* poem, with a small alteration:

> 富士の奥山  
> 今日越えて  
> 浅き夢見じ  
> 酔ひもせず

> Today I cross  
> Fuji's mountain depths;  
> I shall dream no fleeting dreams,  
> Nor be intoxicated.
