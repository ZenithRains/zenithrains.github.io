---
title: "Garibaldi: heroic character and cross-ideological appeal"
date: 2026-09-20T17:20:00+08:00
translationKey: update-garibaldi
---

Garibaldi’s most enduring appeal lies in a heroic character older than the modern division between left and right: **courage, loyalty, adventure, self-discipline, honour, action and sacrifice, together with restraint in the pursuit of personal power and wealth.** He often bore the risks of his convictions himself: exile, expeditions, wounds, and fighting against overwhelming odds. After acquiring immense prestige and real power, he did not turn victory into a dynasty or prolonged personal rule. This gave his character a rare capacity to speak across ideological boundaries. British aristocrats could see *honour*, *duty*, and *disinterestedness* in him; workers and liberals saw *liberty*; American republicans saw the *citizen-soldier* and Cincinnatus; Italian nationalists saw *patria*. They may not have admired the same Garibaldi, yet they recognised a shared character: **someone who asks a community to make sacrifices first takes on greater sacrifices himself; someone capable of winning power can still restrain his desire to possess it after victory.** Thus, although the media and nation-states continually shaped the Garibaldi myth, it was not invented out of nothing. His actual life already came remarkably close to embodying the ancient heroic archetype as nineteenth-century Europe understood it.
